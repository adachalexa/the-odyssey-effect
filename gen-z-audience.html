Chapter II

Gen Z Audience Motivations & Cultural Drivers

Why did younger audiences, raised on streaming, show up for The Odyssey in theaters, and what did they want from it?

Overview

Studios often say younger audiences prefer streaming to theaters. The Odyssey drew a young crowd anyway. We looked at 13 sources, including Variety and The Hollywood Reporter, profiles of IMAX fans, and Reddit discussions, to understand why.

We asked three questions:

1. What got younger people in the door: the director, the IMAX format, or the story?
2. Did a growing taste for things “made the hard way” (a reaction to AI-generated content) play a part?
3. Where did interest fall short?

1. Nolan and the IMAX event got them in the door

Younger audiences mostly came for the director and the feeling of an event.

Moviegoer surveys named Christopher Nolan as the top reason to attend, and one 27-year-old fan called him “a household name for my generation.” IMAX made it feel like an event: only 41 IMAX 70mm screens exist and tickets went on sale a year ahead, so fans said they feared missing out.

2. The story sent people back to Homer

Interest spilled out of theaters into books, audiobooks and even language learning.

Spotify audiobook listening for The Odyssey was up 240% in June, before release, jumped 500% on opening day, and was running about 680% higher after the premiere. U.S. print sales of The Odyssey were up 76% over last year, and searches for “learn Greek” rose about 5,000% in the two weeks after release. On Reddit, the most-upvoted comment in a books thread (about 1,900 upvotes) was about the movie sending people to the book.

3. “Made the hard way” is a big theme in the press, but we need more proof from viewers

News outlets link the film’s success to young people tiring of AI-generated content, but we found little direct evidence from viewers.

Euronews, The Guardian and The Hollywood Reporter tied the film’s success to younger audiences pushing back on what Nolan has credited them with calling “AI slop,” and wanting hands-on, practical filmmaking. None of the Reddit comments we collected named anti-AI feelings as a reason to see the film.

4. It is not only a Gen Z story

The data supports “under-35 audiences, with Gen Z a large and visible part,” not “a Gen Z phenomenon.”

Most youth numbers combine ages 18 to 34, which includes younger Millennials. The only Gen Z-specific number we have is from Spotify, and it shows Millennials listening more than Gen Z. Some Reddit users also pushed back on the “Gen Z” label itself.

5. Where interest fell short

Not everyone loved the film, and not everyone could get in.

Four limits showed up in audience sources:

  • Hype backlash. In one Reddit thread about being underwhelmed, about half of the 106 comments agreed. Overhype, dialogue and pacing were the main complaints, while the visuals were widely praised.
  • Closer reading. In a film-fan thread, the most-upvoted comments judged the final fight against Homer’s text. The film also made some viewers more critical, not less.
  • A values gap. One Gen Z essayist in India’s The Wire argued that the film’s ideas of home, reverence and guilt feel like those of older generations.
  • Access and cost. Ticket-site crashes, long drives and “priced out” complaints show that scarcity left out some of the fans it excited.

Things to keep in mind

  • These findings come from 13 sources, mostly news articles plus three Reddit threads and one essay. This is not a survey of Gen Z.
  • Most “youth” numbers cover ages 18 to 34, not Gen Z alone.
  • Outlets report slightly different numbers (for example, a $124.5M vs. $123.5M domestic opening, or 94% vs. 95% on Rotten Tomatoes). We used the most recent actual figures where we could.
  • This round has no direct TikTok or Letterboxd data. The Letterboxd record comes from press coverage.
  • Reddit blocked automated collection on some threads, so vote counts are missing for one of them.
