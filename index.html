The Odyssey Effect
I. Craft II. Audience III. Cast IV. Social V. Marketing Summary

Five areas of research on one film's success

What made The Odyssey work

The Odyssey (2026) became a worldwide box office hit while breaking from the usual blockbuster playbook: tickets on sale a year ahead, the best IMAX screens held for this film, and no early screenings for influencers. This project looks at why it worked, through five areas of research.

Final Findings

The Odyssey was sold as an event, and people treated it like one.

Across five areas of research, one pattern kept showing up. The film was hard to get (only 41 theaters in the world can show it in IMAX 70mm), came with a strong story about how it was made, and had Christopher Nolan’s name behind it. People sought out premium screens, filled the internet with praise and arguments, and stayed with the film longer than a typical big movie. Skipping influencer screenings did not hurt early trust.

  • Its second-weekend earnings fell 27%, compared with 60% for Spider-Man: Brand New Day.
  • IMAX earned an estimated $534 million worldwide, the most of any IMAX release.
  • Half of the opening audience was 18 to 34, and the story sent many people back to the book.
  • 81% of TikTok views on cast videos came in the four weeks around release.
