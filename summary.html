Research Summary

What did five areas of research tell us about why The Odyssey worked?

Overview

The Odyssey (2026) did not follow the usual blockbuster plan. Tickets went on sale up to a year early, the best IMAX screens were held for it, and there were no early screenings for influencers. We wanted to understand why it worked.

We studied five areas: how the film was made (Chapter I), who showed up and why (Chapter II), the cast (Chapter III), the online conversation (Chapter IV), and the marketing and release plan (Chapter V). We used reviews, box office reports, Google search trends, news coverage, and posts from YouTube, TikTok, Reddit and X.

This page pulls the main findings together. Follow the chapter links to see the charts and details behind each point.

The big picture

The Odyssey was sold as an event, and people treated it like one.

Across all five areas, the same pattern kept showing up. The film was made to feel rare: shot on real film, shown on a small number of special screens, and on sale a year ahead. Fans responded by planning trips, searching for the nearest IMAX screen, and talking about it online. Once it opened, the audience stayed with it longer than a typical big movie.

The data fits this story, but it shows a pattern and does not prove a cause. See “Things to keep in mind” below.

What we learned

Six main takeaways from the five areas of research.

1. Nolan’s name and the IMAX event brought people in

People said they came for the director and the experience, not for the technical details.

Audience surveys named Christopher Nolan as the top reason to buy a ticket, ahead of the famous cast. Premium screens took 53% of the U.S. opening weekend, and IMAX’s share of U.S. earnings grew from 24% to 36% as the run went on. The behind-the-scenes engineering story, such as the soundproof camera housing, mostly reached people through interviews and explainer posts. Even so, those explainer posts were among the most-liked on social media.

Read more: Chapter I and Chapter II

2. Being hard to get helped, and it also left some people out

Only 41 theaters in the world can show IMAX 70mm, and demand was higher than supply.

Tickets went on sale up to a year early. Every top search connected to “The Odyssey tickets” included the word IMAX, and the fastest-growing searches named cities where people hoped to find a screen. Fans reportedly drove for hours, and some resale tickets reached nearly ten times face value.

But there was a downside. The most-liked critical post said the IMAX focus shortchanges people who do not live near an IMAX screen, and some fans said they would wait for the home release.

Read more: Chapter I and Chapter V

3. The film held its audience, and skipping influencer screenings did not hurt trust

The Odyssey’s earnings fell much more slowly than Spider-Man’s, and early reactions were mostly positive.

The Odyssey’s second weekend earned 27% less than its opening. Spider-Man: Brand New Day’s earned 60% less. Among film fans, 86% of clearly positive or negative early reactions were positive, and complaints about “hype” only came weeks later.

Critics rated the film far higher (93.6 out of 100 on Metacritic) than regular viewers did (59.2), so audiences were more divided than critics. Letterboxd ratings rose from 3.44 to 4.58 stars over about two months.

Read more: Chapter V

4. Attention peaked at release, and star power added to it

Interest in the cast was low most of the year, then surged in the weeks around July 17.

81% of the 391 million TikTok views on cast videos came in the four weeks around release. Zendaya led with 139 million views, and videos naming two of the stars typically drew about seven times the views of videos naming one. Most TikTok attention was spectacle, such as red-carpet fashion (38% of views). Google searches showed curiosity instead, such as how much the actors were paid.

Read more: Chapter III

5. The online conversation was huge, mostly unpaid, and divided

People praised the film and argued about it at the same time, and arguing drew attention.

Our samples added up to 31.1 million YouTube views, 39.4 million TikTok plays and 1.27 million Reddit upvotes. The most-viewed YouTube video was about why people dislike the film. Praise-style wording and negative wording showed up about equally often (10 posts vs. 8). Jokes and explainers spread on their own, although part of the buzz came from a studio-run hashtag contest.

Read more: Chapter IV

6. The story reached beyond the theater, and it is not only a Gen Z story

The film sent people to books and audiobooks, and its audience was broader than Gen Z.

U.S. book sales of The Odyssey rose 76%, Spotify audiobook listening rose 500% on opening day, and searches for “learn Greek” rose about 5,000% in the two weeks after release. Half of the opening audience was 18 to 34, but Millennials were a bigger share of Spotify audiobook listeners (45%) than Gen Z (36%). News coverage links the film’s success to a taste for things “made the hard way,” but we have not yet seen that directly from viewers.

Things to keep in mind

  • These are patterns, not proof of cause. We compared The Odyssey with one rival film (Spider-Man: Brand New Day) and three other IMAX films, and they differ in many ways besides marketing.
  • Our samples are collected posts, reviews and articles, not everything. The numbers are snapshots from September and early October 2026 and will keep changing.
  • Sources report slightly different box office numbers, so each chapter shows where its numbers came from.
  • Many social media details, such as resale prices and travel stories, were reported by users or news outlets and are not independently verified.
  • We measured tone by counting positive and negative words, which is rough.
  • Most youth numbers cover ages 18 to 34, not Gen Z alone.

What we are still working on

  • Finding audience data for Gen Z alone, not just ages 18 to 34.
  • Hearing directly from viewers about the “made the hard way” idea.
  • Adding more comparison films, and comparing the social media numbers with an average film.
  • Confirming box office totals across sources.
