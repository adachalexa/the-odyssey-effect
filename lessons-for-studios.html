Chapter VI · Draft

Lessons for Studios

If The Odyssey is a blueprint, what could other studios copy?

Overview

The Odyssey earned about $1.7 billion worldwide (The Numbers, late September 2026). Rather than treat it as a one-off, we ask a practical question: what could other studios copy?

Below are four possible lessons. For each one we show what our research found, what we propose, and what we still need to check.

Draft for discussion. Lessons 1 to 3 lean on our data, though not fully. Lesson 4 and the questions at the end are proposals we have not yet tested. Proposals are not findings.

Lesson 1. Make the format the event

Supported by our data

Reserve wide theatrical runs for films designed for premium large screens, so the theater visit cannot be copied at home.

Why it matters: Premium screens are the one part of the experience a living-room setup cannot match.

What this shows: The film with the most elaborate format story earned about 30% of its money from IMAX, vs. about 20% for similar films and about 7% for a film that used IMAX mainly in its marketing.

Four films cannot prove the format caused this, and The Odyssey is also a mythic spectacle by a famous director. One popular post said the writer would wait for the home release, which will reportedly not keep the film’s full IMAX picture.

Still needed: average ticket prices for premium vs. standard screens (we do not yet know whether audiences paid more).

Lesson 2. Give familiar stories to a director with real ownership

Partly supported

The top reason people gave for attending was the director, and the film was not part of a franchise.

Why it matters: It shows whether audiences are making room for films outside a franchise.

What this shows: Non-franchise films went from 1 of the top 13 releases in 2025 to 5 of the top 10 so far in 2026.

Surveys named Nolan as the top reason to attend. But the franchise sequel Spider-Man: Brand New Day earned more worldwide ($2.49B vs. $1.725B), so franchises are not failing.

Still needed: how the film was greenlit and how much creative control the director had. This would come from industry interviews, not box office data.

Lesson 3. Protect the theatrical window

Partly supported

Scarcity created urgency: limited screens, tickets on sale up to a year early, and an IMAX screen exclusive.

Signs that buyers felt they had to go now

As reported by trade press and social media. Most are not independently verified.

Why it matters: Urgency is what makes people buy a ticket now instead of waiting.

What this shows: Buyers waited in long online queues, paid big resale premiums, and filled overnight showtimes, which is what urgency looks like.

The film’s exclusive window was an IMAX screen exclusive of about three weeks, not a streaming window.

Still needed: how long the gap between theaters and streaming was for The Odyssey and for comparison films. The idea of a 75 to 90+ day window is untested here.

Lesson 4. Rebuild the middle tier

Proposal, not yet tested

Use big-event profits to fund smaller films with the same premium positioning: sharp writing, a distinct look, and protected windows.

How much each film’s second weekend fell

Same source and method for both films (The Numbers).

The Odyssey

Spider-Man: Brand New Day

Why it matters: Films that keep their audience for weeks are the ones that turn a profit without a huge opening.

What this shows: The Odyssey lost about a quarter of its earnings in its second weekend. Spider-Man lost about 60%.

This shows staying power, not why it happened, and it is a very large film. It tells us little yet about $15M to $40M films.

Still needed: earnings, budgets and break-even points for a set of mid-budget thrillers and horror films, plus any reporting on how studios plan their release slates.

What we still need to collect

  • Average ticket prices for premium vs. standard screens.
  • Streaming window lengths for The Odyssey and comparison films.
  • Box office and budget data for mid-budget thrillers and horror films.
  • Reporting on how revenue is split between studios and theaters.

Things to keep in mind

  • One hit cannot prove a template. These lessons are proposals drawn from one film, plus one comparison film and three IMAX films.
  • The Odyssey is both a Nolan film and an IMAX spectacle, so it is hard to say which part drove which result.
  • Box office numbers differ slightly across sources, so each chapter shows where its numbers came from.
