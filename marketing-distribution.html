Chapter V

Non-Traditional Marketing & Distribution Strategy

What happens when a big movie skips influencer promotion and instead sells limited, premium tickets directly to fans?

Overview

Most big movies spend months building hype through influencers and brand partnerships. The Odyssey did it differently. Tickets went on sale up to a year early, the best IMAX screens were held for this film alone, and Universal sent the movie to professional critics instead of holding early screenings for influencers.

To see what that choice changed, we compared The Odyssey with Spider-Man: Brand New Day, a movie that used the opposite approach: lots of brand partnerships and early influencer buzz. We asked two questions:

1. Does selling tickets a year early keep people interested, or wear them out before opening night?
2. If influencers do not get early screenings, do audiences trust the movie less?

1. The movie kept its audience longer

Spider-Man had the bigger opening, but The Odyssey held on to its audience much longer.

Big movies usually earn most of their money in the first weekend and then fade fast. The Odyssey faded slowly. Its second weekend earned about 27% less than its opening, while Spider-Man’s second weekend earned about 60% less. The Odyssey also stayed in about 3,900 U.S. theaters for more than eight weeks, which is unusually steady.

Part of the reason was IMAX. It became the first film to earn more than $500 million from IMAX screens alone, and in India nearly half of advance tickets (48%) were for IMAX.

2. Interest built early and faded slowly

Online searches for The Odyssey started climbing before Spider-Man’s and held up better after the peak.

We tracked how often people searched for each film on Google. Spider-Man’s bigger campaign made a taller peak (100 vs. 81 on the chart below). But nine weeks after its peak, The Odyssey was still at about 7% of its peak interest, compared with 4% for Spider-Man.

People were hunting for access

Every one of the top searches connected to “The Odyssey tickets” included the word IMAX, and the fastest-growing searches named cities such as Manchester, Singapore and Dubai. People were not just learning the film existed. They were looking for where they could see it on the biggest screen. Daily visits to the film’s Wikipedia page went from about 22,000 in July 2025 to 1.2 million on July 18, 2026, and were still nearly double the starting level in late September.

It was not total online silence

The idea of “no online marketing” is not quite right. Universal did post official trailers online, so the rollout happened in stages.

3. Critics loved it, and audiences were more divided

Critics rated The Odyssey far above audiences. For Spider-Man, it was the other way around.

On Metacritic, critics gave The Odyssey an average of 93.6 out of 100, while regular viewers averaged 59.2. Spider-Man’s critics averaged 69.6 and its viewers 76.9. The Odyssey’s audience was not lukewarm. It was split: about 36% of viewer scores were very low (0 to 4 out of 10) and about 54% were high.

4. Skipping influencer screenings did not hurt early trust

Among film fans, the first reactions were mostly positive, and complaints about “hype” only showed up weeks later.

One worry about skipping influencer screenings is that early viewers would feel confused or suspicious. We looked at early reactions (through July 31, 2026) on Letterboxd, Reddit and Metacritic. Of the posts that were clearly positive or negative, 86% were positive on both Letterboxd and Reddit. Words like “overhyped” or “review bomb” were rare overall (1% to 3% of posts), and on Metacritic every one of them came in August and September, weeks after release.

5. Two different ways to make money

Both films earned far more than they cost, so there is more than one way to win.

The Odyssey paid directly for its marketing, about $125 million, and relied on scarcity: limited IMAX screens and early ticket sales. Spider-Man spent very little on traditional TV ads ($3.6 million). Instead, about 165 brand partners generated promotion that was worth an estimated $309 million.

Things to keep in mind

  • We compared two films. They also differ in other ways (an original story vs. a franchise sequel, different casts and release dates), so the results show a pattern, not proof that marketing caused it.
  • Spider-Man lost IMAX screens to The Odyssey’s exclusive window, which may explain part of its faster drop.
  • We measured tone by counting words like “amazing” or “boring,” which misses sarcasm. The early Letterboxd posts mostly came from film enthusiasts.
  • Sources report slightly different box office totals, and all numbers are snapshots from late September 2026 that will keep changing.
