Chapter I

Technical Craft & Analog Cinematic Experience

Does telling the story of how The Odyssey was made, with real film and rare IMAX cameras, help sell premium tickets?

Overview

Christopher Nolan shot The Odyssey on real film with IMAX cameras, which is rare in an age of digital filming. Special equipment was built for it, including a soundproof housing so the loud film camera could get close to actors. Only 41 theaters in the world can show the film in its largest film format, called IMAX 70mm.

We asked two questions:

1. Does highlighting the handmade craft make the film feel rare enough that people will pay more to see it?
2. Does online talk about making the film on real film make the theater visit feel like something you cannot repeat at home?

To find out, we read 992 reviews, about 1,000 Reddit posts and comments, and 510 posts from X, TikTok and Instagram. We also collected box office numbers for IMAX and other premium screens, and compared The Odyssey with three other films.

1. Premium screens brought in a big share of the money

More people chose IMAX and other premium screens than for most films, and that share grew as the run went on.

Premium large-format screens took 53% of the U.S. opening weekend. IMAX alone is estimated to have earned $534 million worldwide by day 73, more than any IMAX release in history. In the U.S., IMAX’s share of the film’s earnings climbed from 24% on opening weekend to 36% by day 73. In plain terms, IMAX audiences kept showing up even as the rest of the audience faded.

Most of the IMAX money came from regular IMAX screens, not the rare film-based ones. By August 9, the 41 IMAX 70mm theaters had earned $37.5 million, about 13% of IMAX’s total.

2. People said they came for Nolan, not for the camera technology

The engineering story is well documented, but it shows up in interviews, not in how ordinary viewers talk.

In an opening-weekend survey, the top reason people gave for buying a ticket was Christopher Nolan himself, ahead of the famous cast. We also searched reviews and Reddit for the technical terms we were tracking, such as “blimp housing,” “mirror rig” and “natural lighting.” They barely appeared.

They do appear on X, but almost all of those posts are clips of Nolan, Matt Damon and Tom Holland explaining the equipment in interviews, plus fan accounts reposting the clips. Almost no one writes “blimp housing” in a review unless they heard it in an interview first. The one term that did spread widely was “70mm IMAX,” mentioned 497 times across all our sources.

3. Behind-the-scenes posts were the most-liked kind

On social media, “how it was made” and “how it works” posts got the most likes.

We sorted 510 posts about The Odyssey and IMAX by tone. Counting posts, most were neutral. Counting likes, neutral posts got even more of the attention, because they were mostly explainers rather than opinions. Eight of the ten most-liked posts were behind-the-scenes clips, projectionist videos or format explainers, such as how IMAX film travels from the set to the theater. The single most-liked post was a viewer who traveled to Dallas to see the film in IMAX 70mm.

4. The film with the biggest craft story had the biggest IMAX share

Compared with three other films, The Odyssey earned the largest share of its money from IMAX.

To see whether The Odyssey’s IMAX numbers were special, we compared it with three other IMAX-heavy films. Oppenheimer (also by Nolan, also shot on film) and Dune: Part Two each earned about 20% of their money from IMAX. Top Gun: Maverick earned about 7%. The Odyssey earned about 30%.

The Odyssey also had by far the most detailed making-of story: custom cameras, a purpose-built soundproof housing, and a film stock revived for the shoot. That fits the idea that the craft story helped. It does not prove it. The Odyssey is also a large mythic spectacle and might have drawn big IMAX crowds on its own. Notably, the gap was not there on opening weekend (IMAX took about 24% of U.S. earnings for The Odyssey and about 26% for Oppenheimer). It opened up later in the run.

5. Access was limited, and fans went out of their way to get it

Only a few theaters could show the film the way it was shot, and demand was higher than supply.

Only 41 theaters worldwide can show IMAX 70mm (25 in the U.S., none in India). As reported by trade press and fans, shows sold out weeks ahead (New York and Los Angeles were booked through August 19), some resale tickets reached nearly ten times face value, and some theaters added 2:30 a.m. showings. Fans reportedly drove for hours or flew to see it. Outside the U.S., IMAX was about half of India’s earnings over 40 days, and 53% of China’s earnings at its IMAX debut.

The scarcity itself became part of the product, including popcorn buckets shaped like the IMAX camera and framed pieces of film.

Not everyone liked this. The most-liked critical post (about 113,000 likes) said the IMAX focus shortchanges people who do not live near an IMAX screen. Another popular post said the writer would just wait for the home release. The home 4K version will reportedly not keep the film’s full IMAX picture shape, so it will not match the theater.

Things to keep in mind

  • Four films are not enough to prove the craft story caused the higher IMAX share. The Odyssey also differs in genre, scale and timing, and it was an original film rather than a sequel.
  • Many of the scarcity details (resale prices, travel, showtimes) come from social media posts and news reports, and we have not verified every one. Where numbers came from trade press, we say so.
  • Premium-format and survey numbers come from news reports that cite studios and survey firms, not from the original data. The latest numbers (week 12) come from posts that cite studio reports.
  • We scored post tone automatically by counting positive and negative words, which is rough. For example, of 37 posts scored “negative,” only 6 actually criticized the film.
  • The social media posts are a collected sample, not every post.
