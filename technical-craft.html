Chapter I

Technical Craft & Analog Cinematic Experience

Does telling the story of how The Odyssey was made, with real film and rare IMAX cameras, help sell premium tickets?

Overview

Christopher Nolan shot The Odyssey on real film with IMAX cameras, which is rare in an age of digital filming. Special equipment was built for it, including a soundproof housing so the loud film camera could get close to actors. Only 41 theaters in the world can show the film in its largest film format, called IMAX 70mm.

We asked two questions:

1. Does highlighting the handmade craft make the film feel rare enough that people will pay more to see it?
2. Does online talk about making the film on real film make the theater visit feel like something you cannot repeat at home?

To find out, we read 992 reviews, Reddit posts and comments, and 510 posts from X, TikTok and Instagram. We also collected box office numbers for IMAX and other premium screens, and compared The Odyssey with three other films.

1. Premium screens brought in a big share of the money

IMAX and other premium screens earned far more than their share of screens would suggest, and their share grew as the run went on.

Premium large-format screens took 53% of the U.S. opening weekend. IMAX alone is estimated to have earned $534 million worldwide by day 73, more than any IMAX release in history. In the U.S., the share of the film’s earnings that came from IMAX climbed from 24% on opening weekend to 36% by day 73.

2. People said they came for Nolan, not for the camera technology

The engineering story is well documented, but it shows up in interviews, not in how ordinary viewers talk.

In an opening-weekend survey, the top reason people gave for buying a ticket was Christopher Nolan himself, ahead of the famous cast.

3. Behind-the-scenes posts were the most-liked kind

On social media, “how it was made” and “how it works” posts got the most likes.

We sorted 510 posts about The Odyssey and IMAX by tone. Most were neutral, and neutral posts earned an even bigger share of the likes, because they were mostly explainers rather than opinions. The single most-liked post was a viewer who traveled to Dallas to see the film in IMAX 70mm.

4. The film with the biggest craft story ended with the biggest IMAX share

On opening weekend The Odyssey looked like other IMAX films. Over the full run, it pulled ahead.

To see whether The Odyssey’s IMAX numbers were special, we compared it with three other IMAX-heavy films. Oppenheimer (also by Nolan, also shot on film) and Dune: Part Two each earned about 20% of their money from IMAX over their runs. Top Gun: Maverick earned about 7%. The Odyssey earned about 30%.

IMAX share of earnings: opening weekend vs. full run

Four films, two points in time.

What this shows: On opening weekend, The Odyssey (24%), Oppenheimer (26%) and Dune: Part Two (22%) were within a few points of each other. The Odyssey pulled ahead later (about 30% vs. about 20%). If the craft story helped, it helped over time, not on day one.

Opening-weekend shares are U.S. only. Full-run shares are worldwide (The Odyssey as of day 73; the others are final). Oppenheimer’s was reported as 20–21%.

The Odyssey also had by far the most detailed making-of story: custom cameras, a purpose-built soundproof housing, and a film stock revived for the shoot. That fits the idea that the craft story helped, but four films cannot prove it. The Odyssey is also a large mythic spectacle and might have drawn big IMAX crowds on its own.

5. Access was limited, and fans went out of their way to get it

Only a few theaters could show the film the way it was shot, and demand was higher than supply.

Only 41 theaters worldwide can show IMAX 70mm. Trade press and fans reported that shows sold out weeks ahead (New York and Los Angeles were booked through August 19), that some resale tickets reached nearly ten times face value, and that some theaters added 2:30 a.m. showings. Fans reportedly drove for hours or flew to see it.

Not everyone liked this. The most-liked critical post (about 113,000 likes) said the IMAX focus shortchanges people who do not live near an IMAX screen. Another popular post said the writer would just wait for the home release, which will reportedly not keep the film’s full IMAX picture shape.

Things to keep in mind

  • Four films are not enough to prove the craft story caused the higher IMAX share. The Odyssey also differs in genre, scale and timing, and it was an original film rather than a sequel.
  • Many of the scarcity details (resale prices, travel, showtimes, India) come from social media posts and news reports that we have not verified. Numbers from trade press are marked as such.
  • Premium-format and survey numbers come from news reports that cite studios and survey firms, not from the original data. The latest numbers (week 12) come from posts that cite studio reports.
  • We scored post tone automatically by counting positive and negative words, which is rough. For example, of 37 posts scored “negative,” only 6 actually criticized the film.
  • The social media posts are a collected sample, not every post.
