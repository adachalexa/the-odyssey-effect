Chapter IV

Viral Social Media Dynamics & Online Sentiment

How did people talk about The Odyssey online, and what did that conversation look like?

Overview

The Odyssey set off a huge amount of unpaid online conversation. We studied YouTube, TikTok and Reddit to see how people shared the film, praised it and argued about it.

Our sample was 73 YouTube videos, 100 TikTok videos, 100 Reddit posts, and 41 Reddit discussion threads with 677 comments. We collected it between September 21 and 30, 2026, covering talk from the film’s announcement in late 2024 through September 2026.

1. The conversation was big and mostly unpaid

Most of the talk came from ordinary viewers and commentators, spread across many communities.

The YouTube videos drew 31.1 million views and about 180,000 comments. The TikTok videos drew 39.4 million plays and about 256,000 shares. The Reddit posts earned 1.27 million upvotes and 135,500 comments. Reddit talk was spread across more than 30 different communities, not one central film forum.

One part was seeded by the studio: a hashtag contest called #TikTokOdysseyContest, which mixed fan videos with promotional “get your tickets” posts. So some of the buzz was incentivized rather than spontaneous.

2. Each platform played a different role

TikTok was fast and shareable, YouTube was long and argumentative, and Reddit mixed debate with jokes.

The timing matters too. TikTok chatter started in December 2024 and stayed low through most of 2025, then spiked in July 2026. YouTube was almost entirely a single week of reviews, the pattern you see when critics drive word of mouth.

3. Praise and backlash showed up together

Some of the most-viewed content was about people disliking the film, and arguing drew attention.

The most-viewed YouTube video in our sample (4.88 million views) was titled “Dumb reasons why people hate the Odyssey.” Another, “Why People Are Hating Nolan’s The Odyssey?”, drew 2.37 million views and 13,645 comments. People also argue in the replies of negative reviews: two of the most negative-leaning reviews drew about one comment for every 100 views, well above normal for this sample.

At the same time there was sincere appreciation, including a TikTok about composer Ludwig Göransson’s score, which used ancient instruments and 35 bronze gongs, and the film’s most-shared TikTok (81,200 shares), which praised its filmmaking.

Reddit’s official discussion thread had a 91% upvote ratio but 16,670 comments, which suggests broad approval with a lot of argument about the reasons. The film was also a huge box office success, so the debate did not stop people from buying tickets.

4. Casting debates became their own conversation

Talk about casting and representation was separate from talk about whether the film was good.

Videos about casting drew far more views than a typical review video. Reddit threads also documented an organized group that criticized the casting months before release, and many other Reddit users pushed back, arguing that the backlash was about politics, not about the film.

5. Jokes and explainers spread on their own

People used the film as material for humor and for learning.

On Reddit, jokes that poked fun at the film’s details out-scored most serious reviews. One joke about believing the journey took 10 years earned 48,156 upvotes, and the top-scoring post overall was a joke about watching the film in IMAX (78,618 upvotes). On TikTok, plot explainers like “The Odyssey in 60 Seconds” treated the film as a study aid for Homer, and self-help style videos reframed Odysseus’s journey as a question about your own life.

6. Real discussion happened too

Some Reddit threads read like film-class discussions.

One thread with 732 upvotes debated the Scylla and Charybdis scene as a moral dilemma, and another compared the film with the 1954 movie Ulysses. A superfan who had seen the film ten times in IMAX 70mm started a thread on adaptation theory. Two similarly upvoted replies often disagreed directly under the same post. One said it “kinda sucked,” and another said “I disagree completely.” That is a sign the disagreement was live, not just two camps shouting past each other.

Things to keep in mind

  • We looked at samples (73 YouTube videos, 100 TikToks, 100 Reddit posts, 41 threads), not every post. The numbers were collected September 21 to 30, 2026, and keep growing.
  • Counting praise and criticism words is rough. One joke post or one contrarian title can swing a small sample.
  • Views and upvotes show what was big, not whether people loved or hated it.
  • Some of the volume was seeded by a studio hashtag contest, so not all of it was spontaneous.
  • We have not yet compared these numbers with an average film, so we cannot say how unusual they are.
  • Our Reddit search returned some off-topic results. Only posts confirmed to be about this film were used in the deeper comment analysis.
  • We did not find a Barbenheimer-style pairing with a rival release. The conversation about The Odyssey stood on its own.
